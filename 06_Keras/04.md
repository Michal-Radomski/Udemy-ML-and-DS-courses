Softmax, sigmoid, and ReLU are all activation functions used in neural networks, but they serve different purposes and behave
very differently.
[codefriends](https://www.codefriends.net/en/courses/ai-fundamentals-deep-learning/chapter-1/activation-functions-comparison)

---

### What problem each one solves

- **ReLU**: Introduces non‑linearity in hidden layers; it keeps positive values and sets negatives to zero, which speeds up
  training and helps avoid vanishing gradients in deep networks.
  [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/activation-functions-101-sigmoid-tanh-relu-softmax-ben-hammouda)
- **Sigmoid**: Maps any real number to a value between 0 and 1, so it is usually used for **binary classification**
  (single‑output “probability‑like” score).
  [codefriends](https://www.codefriends.net/courses/ai-fundamentals-deep-learning/chapter-1/activation-functions-comparison)
- **Softmax**: Also maps values to probabilities, but normalizes a **vector** so all outputs are between 0 and 1 and sum to
  1; used for **multi‑class classification** (e.g., class A, B, C).
  [community.deeplearning](https://community.deeplearning.ai/t/why-relu-and-softmax/58262)

---

### Output range and shape

- **ReLU**: output is \([0, \infty)\); it’s piecewise linear with a sharp kink at 0.
  [codefriends](https://www.codefriends.net/en/courses/ai-fundamentals-deep-learning/chapter-1/activation-functions-comparison)
- **Sigmoid**: output is \((0,1)\); it’s smooth, S‑shaped, and saturates for large positive/negative inputs.
  [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/activation-functions-101-sigmoid-tanh-relu-softmax-ben-hammouda)
- **Softmax**: output vector entries are \((0,1)\) and sum to 1, so it always represents a probability distribution over
  classes. [community.deeplearning](https://community.deeplearning.ai/t/why-relu-and-softmax/58262)

---

### Where they are typically used

- **ReLU**: Almost always in **hidden layers** of modern deep networks (CNNs, MLPs, etc.).
  [geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/machine-learning/activation-functions-neural-networks/)
- **Sigmoid**: Often in the **output layer** for **binary classification**; sometimes in hidden layers in older models.
  [geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/deep-learning/choosing-the-right-activation-function-for-your-neural-network/)
- **Softmax**: Almost always in the **output layer** for **multi‑class classification** (mutually exclusive classes).
  [towardsdatascience](https://towardsdatascience.com/activation-functions-in-neural-networks-how-to-choose-the-right-one-cb20414c04e5/)

---

### Key differences at a glance

| Aspect          | ReLU                         | Sigmoid                              | Softmax                                   |
| --------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| Output range    | \([0,\infty)\)               | \((0,1)\) node                       | \((0,1)\) per class, sum to 1             |
| Typical use     | Hidden layers                | Binary‑output layer                  | Multi‑class‑output layer                  |
| Non‑linearity   | Piecewise linear, sharp at 0 | Smooth, S‑shaped                     | Smooth, exponential over multiple outputs |
| Gradient issues | “Dying ReLU” for negatives   | Vanishing gradients for large inputs | Same, but mitigated by cross‑entropy loss |

[codefriends](https://www.codefriends.net/courses/ai-fundamentals-deep-learning/chapter-1/activation-functions-comparison)

In short: use **ReLU inside the network**, **sigmoid at the output for yes/no**, and **softmax at the output for “which one
of many classes.”** [geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/machine-learning/activation-functions-neural-networks/)
