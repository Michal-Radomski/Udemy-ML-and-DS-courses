In time series modeling and statistics, **LLF (Log-Likelihood Function)** and **AIC (Akaike Information Criterion)** are core
metrics used to evaluate how well a model fits your data and to help choose the best model parameters.

Here is what each one means and how they work together:

---

### 1. LLF (Log-Likelihood or Log-Likelihood Function)

- **What it is:** Likelihood measures how probable it is to have observed your actual time series data, given the specific
  parameters of your model. Because actual probabilities can result in extremely small numbers, statisticians take the
  natural logarithm, giving us the **Log-Likelihood (LLF)**.
- **How to interpret it:**
- LLF values are typically negative numbers (e.g., $-150.4$ vs. $-250.2$).
- **Higher (closer to zero or more positive) is better.** A higher LLF means the model's parameters make the observed data
  points much more likely to have occurred.

- **The Catch:** If you keep adding more parameters to a time series model (like increasing $p$ and $q$ in an ARIMA model),
  the LLF will almost always improve because the model becomes more flexible. However, this can lead to **overfitting**,
  where the model memorizes past noise rather than true patterns.

---

### 2. AIC (Akaike Information Criterion)

- **What it is:** AIC solves the overfitting problem of LLF. It is a mathematical score that balances **model fit** (using
  LLF) against **model complexity** (the number of parameters used).
- **The Formula Concept:**

$$\text{AIC} = 2k - 2(\text{LLF})$$

_(Where $k$ is the number of parameters in the model, and LLF is the log-likelihood)._

- The $2k$ term acts as a **penalty** for adding extra parameters.

- **How to interpret it:**
- **Lower is better.**
- A good model has a high LLF (good fit) achieved with a low $k$ (simplicity).
- When comparing multiple models (like different ARIMA configurations), the model with the **lowest AIC** is considered the
  optimal balance of accuracy and simplicity.

---

### How They Work Together in AutoARIMA

When an algorithm like AutoARIMA evaluates dozens of different model combinations, it calculates the LLF to see how well each
one fits the historical data, plugs it into the AIC formula with a penalty for complexity, and ultimately selects the model
that minimizes the AIC score.
