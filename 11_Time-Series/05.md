In time series analysis and forecasting, a **random walk** is a process where a variable takes a step based on its previous
value plus a random shock (which is essentially white noise).

Think of it as a journey where every step you take is determined by a random event starting from _wherever you currently
are_, rather than from a fixed starting point.

### The Classic Analogy: The Drunkard's Walk

Imagine someone stumbling home. From their current position, they take a random step forward or backward. Their next step
doesn't depend on where they started the night or where their house is—it depends entirely on **where they are right now**
plus a random stumble. Over time, they might wander far away, or they might end up right back near where they started, purely
by chance.

### How It Relates to White Noise

If you remember that **white noise** consists of purely random, independent data points with no memory, a random walk is
essentially what happens when you **accumulate** white noise over time:

- **White Noise:** Today's value is completely random and resets tomorrow.
- **Random Walk:** Today's value is yesterday's value _plus_ today's white noise shock.

### Why Random Walks Matter in Forecasting

- **Non-Stationary Data:** Unlike white noise, a random walk doesn't hover around a stable average. It can "drift" and wander
  off indefinitely, meaning its statistical properties change over time.
- **The Best Forecast is Today's Value:** Because the next step is entirely unpredictable, the best guess for tomorrow's
  value in a pure random walk is simply **whatever the value is today**. You cannot forecast long-term trends because there
  are no stable trends—just random drifting.
- **Financial Markets:** Stock prices, currency exchange rates, and crypto values are often modeled as random walks. This is
  why consistently predicting short-term stock price movements is so notoriously difficult!
