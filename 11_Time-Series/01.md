## What is Time Series?

A **time series** is a collection of data points recorded over a period of time, usually at regular intervals (daily, weekly,
monthly, or yearly).

In simple words, **time series means observing how something changes over time.**

### Example: Monthly Sales of a Shop

Imagine you own a shop and record its sales every month.

| Month    | Sales (€) |
| -------- | --------- |
| January  | 1,000     |
| February | 1,200     |
| March    | 1,500     |
| April    | 1,300     |
| May      | 1,800     |
| June     | 2,000     |

This is a time series because:

- We collect data over time.
- Each data point has a specific time.
- We can observe how sales increase or decrease.

### Real-Life Examples of Time Series

1. **Weather:** Daily temperature measurements.
2. **Stock Market:** Daily stock prices.
3. **Business:** Monthly sales or yearly profits.
4. **Healthcare:** Daily heart rate measurements.
5. **Economics:** Annual inflation rates.
6. **Education:** Students' monthly exam scores.

### Why Do We Use Time Series?

Time series analysis helps us:

- Understand past trends.
- Identify patterns.
- Predict future values.
- Make better decisions.

**For example:** If a shop's sales increase every month, time series analysis can help predict its sales for the next month.

### Important Point to Remember

A time series has two main components:

- **Time:** When the data was collected (January, February, March).
- **Value:** What was measured (sales, temperature, stock price).

**In one sentence:** Time series is data collected over time to understand patterns, trends, and predict future outcomes.
