**ARIMA** and **SARIMAX** are powerful statistical models used for **time series forecasting**—meaning they predict future
values based on past patterns, trends, and seasonality (like predicting next month's sales, daily website traffic, or
temperature).

Here is a breakdown of what they stand for and how they work:

---

### 1. ARIMA (AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average)

ARIMA is the foundational model for forecasting data that changes over time. It combines three core components, represented
by the parameters **$(p, d, q)$**:

- **AR (AutoRegressive - $p$):** Uses the dependency between an observation and a number of lagged (past) observations.
  _Example: If sales were high the last two months, they might be high this month too._
- **I (Integrated - $d$):** Uses differencing of raw observations (subtracting an observation from an immediate predecessor)
  to make a non-stationary time series (one with a rising or falling trend) stationary. Stationarity makes modeling easier
  because the mean and variance stay roughly constant over time.
- **MA (Moving Average - $q$):** Uses the dependency between an observation and a residual error from a moving average model
  applied to lagged observations.

### 2. SARIMAX (Seasonal ARIMA with eXogenous variables)

SARIMAX is an advanced extension of ARIMA designed to handle more complex real-world data patterns:

- **S (Seasonal - $(P, D, Q)_s$):** Adds components specifically for recurring seasonal patterns (e.g., yearly spikes in
  retail sales during December, or weekly dips on weekends).
- **X (eXogenous variables):** Allows you to include **external factors** that influence the target variable. For example, if
  you are forecasting ice cream sales using a SARIMAX model, the external variables ($X$) could include daily temperature,
  rainfall, or marketing spend.
