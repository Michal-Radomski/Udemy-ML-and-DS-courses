In Python's `statsmodels` library, `sts.adfuller` stands for the **Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test**. It is one of the
most popular statistical tests used to determine whether a time series is **stationary** or **non-stationary**.

Building on your previous question about stationarity, remember that many time series models (like ARIMA) require data to be
stationary. The ADF test gives you a rigorous statistical way to check if your data meets that requirement.

### How the ADF Test Works

The test looks at a time series to check for a **unit root**—a mathematical feature that indicates a series is non-stationary
(like a random walk or a series with a trend).

When you run `sts.adfuller(data)`, it sets up a statistical hypothesis test:

- **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** The time series has a unit root (meaning it is **non-stationary**).
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$):** The time series has no unit root (meaning it is **stationary**).

### Interpreting the Results

When you call the function, it returns several values, but the two most important ones to look at are:

1. **The ADF Test Statistic:** A negative number. The more negative this number is, the stronger the evidence against the
   null hypothesis (and the more likely your data is stationary).
2. **The p-value:**

- **Low p-value (typically $\le 0.05$):** You reject the null hypothesis. This means your data **is stationary** and ready
  for forecasting models.
- **High p-value ($> 0.05$):** You fail to reject the null hypothesis. This means your data **is non-stationary**, and you
  will likely need to apply a transformation (like differencing) before modeling it.

### Quick Code Example

```python
import statsmodels.tsa.stattools as sts
import pandas as pd

# Assuming 'df['prices']' is your time series data
result = sts.adfuller(df['prices'])

print(f"ADF Statistic: {result[0]}")
print(f"p-value: {result[1]}")

```
