Gradient vanishing and exploding are common issues during backpropagation in deep neural networks, where gradients become too
small or too large as they propagate backward through layers.
[geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/deep-learning/vanishing-and-exploding-gradients-problems-in-deep-learning/)

## Vanishing Gradient

This occurs when gradients shrink exponentially to near zero in early layers due to repeated multiplication of small
derivatives (e.g., from sigmoid or tanh activations, where values are <1). Early layers learn very slowly or stop updating,
hindering training of deep networks or long sequences in RNNs.
[geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/deep-learning/vanishing-and-exploding-gradients-problems-in-deep-learning/)

## Exploding Gradient

The opposite happens when gradients grow exponentially large, often from weights >1 or high learning rates, causing drastic
weight updates, loss divergence, and unstable training.
[geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/deep-learning/vanishing-and-exploding-gradients-problems-in-deep-learning/)

## Key Causes

- Activation functions with saturated derivatives (sigmoid/tanh for vanishing).
  [geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/deep-learning/vanishing-and-exploding-gradients-problems-in-deep-learning/)
- Poor weight initialization or deep architectures amplifying multiplications.
  [neptune](https://neptune.ai/blog/monitoring-diagnosing-and-solving-gradient-issues-in-foundation-models)
- Especially severe in RNNs for long-term dependencies.
  [geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/deep-learning/vanishing-and-exploding-gradients-problems-in-deep-learning/)

## Solutions

Common fixes include ReLU activations, better initialization (Xavier/He), gradient clipping, batch/layer normalization, and
residual connections.
[neptune](https://neptune.ai/blog/monitoring-diagnosing-and-solving-gradient-issues-in-foundation-models)
