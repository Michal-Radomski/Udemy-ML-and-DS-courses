GPUs excel at calculations over CPUs for tasks that benefit from massive parallelism, like matrix operations in AI or
graphics rendering. CPUs, by contrast, prioritize sequential processing and complex logic.

## Core Architecture

GPUs feature thousands of smaller, simpler cores designed for executing many identical operations simultaneously, unlike
CPUs' fewer (typically 4-16) powerful cores optimized for single-threaded tasks. This parallelism allows GPUs to handle
data-intensive workloads, such as multiplying large matrices, far faster by dividing problems into tiny, independent chunks.
CPUs shine in branching logic or low-latency decisions but bottleneck on repetitive math across huge datasets.

## Key Applications

GPUs dominate in machine learning by accelerating backpropagation and similar ops, often reducing training time from days to
hours compared to CPUs. They also power graphics rendering and scientific simulations through efficient parallel compute. For
non-parallel work like databases or light inference, CPUs remain more efficient due to lower overhead.
