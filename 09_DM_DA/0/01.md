Survival analysis is a statistical method focused on studying the time until a specific event occurs, such as death, failure,
or recovery. It accounts for incomplete data through censoring, where some subjects haven't experienced the event by the
study's end. [statsandr](https://statsandr.com/blog/what-is-survival-analysis/)

## Core Concepts

It models the **survival function** S(t), the probability of surviving past time t, and the **hazard function**, the
instantaneous risk of the event at time t. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2394262/) Key
challenges include handling censored observations and comparing groups via Kaplan-Meier curves or Cox proportional hazards
models. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10957029/)

## Common Applications

- **Medical research**: Time to patient death or disease recurrence.
  [scikit-survival.readthedocs](https://scikit-survival.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user_guide/understanding_predictions.html)
- **Engineering**: Time until machine failure (reliability analysis).
  [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_analysis)
- **Economics/Sociology**: Duration until unemployment ends or marriage occurs.
  [bmj](https://www.bmj.com/about-bmj/resources-readers/publications/statistics-square-one/12-survival-analysis)

## Example Workflow

For a clinical trial, plot survival curves:

- Collect time-to-event and censoring status.
- Estimate S(t) non-parametrically with Kaplan-Meier.
- Test differences (e.g., log-rank test) and fit semi-parametric models like Cox.
  [cscu.cornell](https://cscu.cornell.edu/wp-content/uploads/surv.pdf)
