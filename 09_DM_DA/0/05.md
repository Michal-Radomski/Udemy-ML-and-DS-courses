**CHAID** (Chi-squared Automatic Interaction Detection) is a decision tree algorithm used for classification and regression
tasks, particularly effective with categorical data. [statistics](https://www.statistics.com/glossary/chaid/)

## How It Works

It builds trees by applying chi-square tests to identify statistically significant splits between predictor variables and the
target outcome. [docs.rapidminer](https://docs.rapidminer.com/latest/studio/operators/modeling/predictive/trees/chaid.html)
Unlike binary-splitting methods like CART, CHAID allows multi-way branches from a single node and merges similar categories
before splitting. [lightly](https://www.lightly.ai/glossary/chi-squared-automatic-interaction-detection-chaid) The process
recurses until no significant splits remain, based on p-value thresholds adjusted for multiple tests (e.g., Bonferroni).
[en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi-square_automatic_interaction_detection)

## Key Applications

CHAID excels in market research for customer segmentation, direct marketing to predict responses, and exploring variable
interactions. [b2binternational](https://www.b2binternational.com/research/methods/faq/chaid-analysis/) It's also used in
medical research and as an interpretable tool to explain black-box models.
[tredence](https://www.tredence.com/blog/leveraging-chaid-decision-trees-in-blackbox-models-for-enhanced-explanations)
Developed by Gordon V. Kass around 1980, it builds on earlier techniques like AID and THAID.
[docs.tibco](https://docs.tibco.com/data-science/GUID-B9440C34-84AD-4D1D-BB25-3EB3E68B3888.html)
