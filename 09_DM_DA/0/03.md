# What is log rank test

The log-rank test is a non-parametric statistical test used in survival analysis to compare the survival distributions (e.g.,
Kaplan-Meier curves) of two or more groups, testing if their hazard rates differ significantly over time.[^1][^3]

## How It Works

It divides the timeline into intervals at each observed event time across groups, then compares **observed events** (O_i)
versus **expected events** (E_i) per group under the null hypothesis of identical survival curves. The test statistic follows
a chi-squared distribution: $\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i}$, with significance via p-value.[^4][^6]

## Key Assumptions

- Independent censoring (censoring unrelated to event risk).
- Events are non-informative (no ties assumed, though handled).
- Proportional hazards not required (unlike Cox model).[^3][^5]

## Applications

Common in clinical trials to assess treatment effects (e.g., drug A vs. placebo survival), epidemiology for risk factors, and
reliability for product failure rates.[^1][^4] In Python's `lifelines`:
`from lifelines.statistics import logrank_test; results = logrank_test(group1_times, group1_events, group2_times, group2_events)`.
