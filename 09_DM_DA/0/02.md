# What is Kaplan-Meyer Estimator

The Kaplan-Meier estimator is a non-parametric statistical method used in survival analysis to estimate the survival function
from lifetime data, particularly when some observations are censored (incomplete). It produces a step function that
represents the probability of surviving past time $t$, calculated as the product of conditional survival probabilities at
each observed event time.[^1][^3]

## Formula

The estimator is given by

$$
\hat{S}(t) = \prod_{t_j \leq t} \left(1 - \frac{d_j}{n_j}\right)
$$

where $t_j$ are the ordered event times, $d_j$ is the number of events at $t_j$, and $n_j$ is the number of subjects at risk
just before $t_j$. Censored cases reduce $n_j$ but don't count toward $d_j$.[^2][^1]

## Key Features

- Handles right-censoring effectively, common in medical trials where follow-up ends before the event.[^3][^5]
- Generates survival curves for visual group comparisons, often tested with log-rank statistics.[^6][^2]
- Assumes no tied event times or independent censoring, but extensions exist for violations.[^1]

## Applications

Used in clinical studies to compare treatments (e.g., time to recovery), reliability engineering for failure times, and
social sciences for event durations like job tenure.[^5][^3] <span style="display:none">[^10][^4][^7][^8][^9]</span>
