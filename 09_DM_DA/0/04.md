# What is Cox Proportional Hazard Regression

Cox proportional hazards regression, often called the Cox model, is a semi-parametric statistical method in survival analysis
for modeling the effect of covariates on the hazard rate of an event occurring over time, like death or failure.[^1][^5]

## Model Formula

The hazard function is

$$
h(t|X) = h_0(t) \exp(\beta_1 X_1 + \cdots + \beta_p X_p)
$$

where $h_0(t)$ is the unspecified baseline hazard, and $\exp(\beta_i)$ gives the hazard ratio (HR) for covariate $X_i$—e.g.,
HR > 1 means higher risk.[^2][^1]

## Key Features

- **Proportional hazards assumption**: Covariate effects are constant over time (hazard ratios don't change), testable via
  Schoenfeld residuals.[^4][^1]
- Handles censoring and time-varying covariates; no baseline hazard form assumed.[^3][^6]
- Outputs coefficients, HRs with confidence intervals, and p-values for significance.[^7]

## Applications

Used in clinical trials to adjust survival for factors like age, treatment, or biomarkers (e.g., HR=1.5 for smokers means 50%
higher death risk).[^8][^1] In Python's `lifelines`:
`from lifelines import CoxPHFitter; cph.fit(df, duration_col='time', event_col='status')`.[^1]
