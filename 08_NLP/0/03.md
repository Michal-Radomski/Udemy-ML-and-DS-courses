Stop words in NLP are the most common words in a language—like "the," "is," "and," or "in"—that carry little semantic value
and are filtered out during text preprocessing to focus on meaningful content.
[milvus](https://milvus.io/ai-quick-reference/what-are-stop-words-in-nlp)

## Purpose

They reduce noise in data, improve efficiency for tasks like search engines, sentiment analysis, or topic modeling, and lower
dimensionality for machine learning models by ignoring grammatical fillers.
[docs.nvidia](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/curator/latest/curate-text/process-data/language-management/stopwords.html)

## Examples

Common English stop words include articles (a, an, the), prepositions (on, at, to), pronouns (I, you, it), and conjunctions
(and, but, or). Libraries like NLTK and spaCy provide predefined lists, customizable per language or domain.
[codesignal](https://codesignal.com/learn/courses/text-data-preprocessing-in-python/lessons/demystifying-stop-words-in-natural-language-processing)

## Usage in Code

With NLTK (from prior context), load and filter:

```
from nltk.corpus import stopwords
stop_words = set(stopwords.words('english'))
filtered = [w for w in tokens if w.lower() not in stop_words]
```

Not always removed—e.g., negation words like "not" may be kept for context in sentiment tasks.
