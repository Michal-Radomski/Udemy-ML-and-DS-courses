Natural Language Processing (NLP) enables computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language. It combines
artificial intelligence, linguistics, and machine learning to process text and speech data effectively.
[aws.amazon](https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/nlp/)

## Core Definition

NLP is a subfield of AI that focuses on the interaction between computers and human language. It allows machines to analyze
unstructured data from sources like emails, social media, and audio for insights such as sentiment or intent. Key components
include computational linguistics for language models and deep learning for pattern recognition.
[iso](https://www.iso.org/artificial-intelligence/natural-language-processing)

## How It Works

NLP systems break down language through steps like tokenization (splitting text into words), stemming (reducing words to
roots), and named entity recognition (identifying names, dates, or places). Machine learning models then interpret meaning,
context, and grammar to enable tasks like translation or summarization.
[ibm](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/natural-language-processing)

## Key Applications

- **Machine Translation**: Powers tools like Google Translate by decoding grammar across languages.
  [coursera](https://www.coursera.org/articles/natural-language-processing)
- **Sentiment Analysis**: Gauges emotions in reviews or posts for business decisions.
  [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/natural-language-processing)
- **Chatbots and Voice Assistants**: Handles real-time speech-to-text and natural responses, as in Siri or Alexa.
  [nnlm](https://www.nnlm.gov/guides/data-glossary/natural-language-processing)
- **Text Generation**: Creates coherent content like reports or emails from prompts.
  [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/natural-language-processing)

NLP drives automation in customer service, data analysis, and content creation, making vast text data actionable.
