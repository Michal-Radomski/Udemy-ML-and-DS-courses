Lemmatization and stemming are key text normalization techniques in natural language processing (NLP) that reduce words to their base or root forms to improve analysis, search, and model performance. [coursera](https://www.coursera.org/articles/lemmatization-vs-stemming)

## Stemming
Stemming uses rule-based algorithms, like the Porter Stemmer, to strip prefixes and suffixes from words, producing a stem that may not be a dictionary word. For example, "running," "runs," and "runner" might all stem to "run," but irregular forms like "better" could become "bet." It's fast and simple but can create non-words and ignores context or part-of-speech (POS). [stackoverflow](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/1787110/what-is-the-difference-between-lemmatization-vs-stemming)

## Lemmatization
Lemmatization maps words to their dictionary base form (lemma) using linguistic knowledge, vocabulary, and context, often requiring POS tagging. For instance, "better" lemmatizes to "good," and "running" (as a verb) becomes "run," while considering grammar for accuracy. It's slower and more resource-intensive but yields valid words with better precision. [baeldung](https://www.baeldung.com/cs/stemming-vs-lemmatization)

## Key Differences
| Aspect          | Stemming                          | Lemmatization                     |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Output         | Stem (may not be real word)      | Lemma (dictionary word)          |
| Method         | Rule-based truncation            | Context-aware with lexicon/POS   |
| Speed          | Faster, simpler                  | Slower, more complex             |
| Accuracy       | Lower, prone to errors           | Higher, linguistically sound     |
| Use Cases      | Search engines, quick indexing   | Chatbots, sentiment analysis    | [coursera](https://www.coursera.org/articles/lemmatization-vs-stemming)
